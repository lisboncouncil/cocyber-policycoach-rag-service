## 1. Purpose

This document sets out [Organisation]'s 10 Golden Rules for Cybersecurity — a concise, accessible set of security behaviours that every employee and contractor must know, understand, and follow. These rules translate [Organisation]'s security policies into everyday actions.

All personnel must acknowledge and agree to abide by these rules as a condition of access to [Organisation]'s systems. These rules support compliance with international cybersecurity standards (security awareness).

---

## 2. Scope

These rules apply to all employees, contractors, consultants, and any other person with access to [Organisation]'s information systems, data, or premises.

---

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

### 3.1 CISO
- Own and maintain this document.
- Ensure rules are communicated to all personnel during onboarding and at least annually.

### 3.2 Department Managers
- Ensure all team members have read, understood, and signed the acknowledgement.
- Reinforce security-aware behaviours within their teams.

### 3.3 All Employees and Contractors
- Read, understand, and apply these rules in daily work.
- Complete mandatory security awareness training.
- Report any suspected breach of these rules immediately.

---

## 4. The 10 Golden Rules

### Rule 1 — Use Strong, Unique Passwords and a Password Manager
Create long passwords or passphrases (minimum [12] characters) that you do not reuse across systems. Store all work credentials in [Organisation]'s approved password manager. Never share passwords with anyone.

*Refer to: Password Policy (POL-006)*

### Rule 2 — Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
Always use MFA where available, especially for email, remote access, and cloud services. MFA stops attackers even if your password is stolen.

*Refer to: Access Control Policy (POL-002)*

### Rule 3 — Keep Software and Devices Up to Date
Install security updates promptly on all devices you use for work. Do not delay or dismiss update notifications. Unpatched systems are the most common way attackers gain access.

*Refer to: Vulnerability and Patch Management Policy (POL-007)*

### Rule 4 — Think Before You Click — Recognise Phishing
Do not click links or open attachments in unexpected emails, even if they appear to come from a colleague or a trusted organisation. Verify unexpected requests by phone or in person before acting. Report suspicious emails to IT immediately.

### Rule 5 — Protect Devices — Lock, Encrypt, and Secure
Lock your screen whenever you leave your device unattended (Win+L / Cmd+Ctrl+Q). Enable full-disk encryption on all [Organisation] devices. Never leave devices unattended in public places or in vehicles.

### Rule 6 — Use Only Approved Tools and Networks
Use only [Organisation]-approved software, applications, and cloud services for work purposes. Avoid free or personal tools for handling [Organisation] data. When working remotely, always connect via [Organisation]'s VPN. Do not use public or unsecured Wi-Fi without VPN.

*Refer to: Network Security Policy (POL-005)*

### Rule 7 — Handle Data with Care — Classify and Protect
Understand the classification of the information you work with. Do not share confidential or personal data with unauthorised parties. Encrypt sensitive data before transmitting it. Dispose of physical documents securely (shred; do not put in general waste).

*Refer to: Asset Management Policy (POL-003)*

### Rule 8 — Back Up Your Work
Ensure your work files are saved in designated, backed-up locations (not only on your local device). If you are unsure whether a folder is backed up, ask IT. Data stored only on a local device may be permanently lost in the event of failure, theft, or ransomware.

*Refer to: Backup and Recovery Policy (POL-004)*

### Rule 9 — Report Incidents and Suspicious Activity — Do Not Stay Silent
If you suspect something is wrong — a system behaving unusually, a suspicious email, a lost device, or a potential breach — report it immediately to IT ([contact details]). Early reporting limits damage. You will not be penalised for reporting a suspected incident in good faith.

*Refer to: Cyber Incident Response Plan (POL-008)*

### Rule 10 — Security is Everyone's Responsibility
Cybersecurity is not only the job of the IT department. Your decisions and behaviours directly affect the security of [Organisation] and your colleagues. Stay curious, ask questions, and participate in security awareness activities. Help colleagues who may be unfamiliar with these rules.

---

## 5. Training and Acknowledgement

- All new employees and contractors must receive security awareness training including these rules during onboarding, prior to being granted access to [Organisation] systems.
- All personnel must complete refresher training at least **annually**.
- Each person must sign the **Staff Acknowledgement Form (Annex A)** confirming they have read, understood, and will abide by these rules.
- Training completion and signed acknowledgements are recorded by [HR / IT] and maintained as documented evidence for ISMS audit purposes.

---

## 6. Monitoring and Review

- Security awareness effectiveness is assessed at least **annually** through training completion rates, phishing simulation results, and incident trends.
- This document is reviewed annually and updated to reflect the current threat landscape and lessons learned from incidents.

---

## 7. Enforcement

Failure to follow these rules may result in disciplinary action in accordance with [Organisation]'s HR policies. Serious or repeated violations may result in revocation of system access, termination of employment or contract, and in cases of criminal activity, referral to law enforcement.
